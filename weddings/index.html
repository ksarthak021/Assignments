Weddings

The wedding, from mandap to last light.

A Jaipur wedding is a sequence, not a single decorated hour. SVTP Events plans the mandap, the sangeet, the baraat, the pheras, and the reception as one house.

Plan the weddingCompare packages

The arc

Six parts of the same day, or the same weekend.

Families keep different rites and different lengths. The structure below is the one the house knows how to hold: a place for each ceremony, and a path between them.

01

Mandap

The mandap is the room inside the room. We draw its height, its four sides, and the way elders sit close enough to witness. Flowers carry the structure. They do not bury it.

02

Sangeet

A sangeet fails when it is only a stage in a field. It wants a ceiling, a front row that can see, and light that flatters both dance and the people watching.

03

Baraat

The procession needs a path with width, shade, and an end. We design the threshold the baraat arrives through, and the pause before the family is received.

04

Pheras

The fire is small and the moment is not. Seating, sound, and the ring around the agni are planned so the vows can be heard and the photographers are not the ceremony.

05

Reception

By evening the tent should feel like another climate. The couple’s entry, the elders’ blessing, and dinner each need a different light and a different volume of music.

06

Palace and tent

Some families marry on palace grounds. Some marry under canvas built for the week. The house works in both, and often in a marriage of the two: stone at the edge, a tent for the rite.

With the family

Direction is part of the wedding, not an extra.

The house asks which rites are essential, who must sit near the fire, and how large the baraat really is. Decoration follows those answers. A beautiful mandap that the family cannot use is a set, not a wedding.

Palace grounds and tents are both part of the practice. Stone gives one kind of room. Canvas gives another, built for the week and taken down after. Many weddings use both.
